# Can you drink tap water in Seoul? The Arisu reality vs. old pipe risks

## 💡 Local Insider Tip Boxes

### 🚰 Arisu Municipal Quality
Seoul's tap water brand 'Arisu' (아리수) is rigorously purified across 6 major water plants and tested for 350 quality indicators—exceeding WHO drinking water guidelines. You can safely brush your teeth and boil water anywhere in the city.

### 🏢 Old Villa & Hanok Caution
If staying in an Airbnb inside a red-brick multi-unit villa built before 1994, the building may still have aging galvanized steel pipes or uncleaned rooftop water tanks. Buy 2-liter bottled water (Samdasoo) at convenience stores for 1,100 KRW for unboiled drinking.

### 🍽️ Restaurant Water Bottles
The stainless-steel water carafes or plastic bottles placed on dining tables in Korean restaurants contain cold filtered water from dedicated multi-stage filtration dispensers (Coway/ChungHo). It is completely clean and complimentary.

## 📖 Article Content

### 1. The Arisu Paradox: World-Class Purification, Low Domestic Consumption

The Seoul Metropolitan Government has invested billions of dollars into advanced membrane filtration and ozone disinfection technology, producing municipal tap water branded as 'Arisu'. Chemical testing consistently proves that Seoul tap water is as clean and mineral-rich as Evian.

Despite this, fewer than 5% of Seoul residents drink unboiled water directly from the kitchen faucet. This national habit stems from the rapid urban industrialization era of the 1980s and 1990s, when aging plumbing created widespread skepticism that persists across generations.

### 2. The Building Vintage Factor: 1994 Plumbing Cutoff

The water leaving the Seoul municipal treatment plant is pristine. However, its quality at your faucet depends entirely on the pipes inside your specific building. In 1994, South Korea banned the use of corrosion-prone galvanized steel water pipes in residential construction.

If you are staying in a modern high-rise hotel or newly constructed officetel (built after 2000), the internal pipes are stainless steel or corrosion-free PVC, making direct tap water safe. If your accommodation is an older walk-up villa in Yeonnam-dong or an unrestored traditional hanok in Bukchon, rust particles and mineral sediment can leach into standing tap water overnight.

### 3. Practical Rules for Travelers

• Teeth Brushing & Face Washing: Completely safe with tap water in every hotel, hostel, and apartment in Korea.

• Boiling for Tea/Ramen: Boiling tap water in an electric kettle eliminates all chlorine aroma and makes it 100% safe to consume.

• Raw Cold Drinking Water: Grab a 2-liter bottle of Jeju Samdasoo (제주 삼다수) or Baeksansoo (백산수) from any convenience store for ~1,100 KRW ($0.80 USD), or use the hot/cold filtered water dispenser located in your hotel lobby or guesthouse kitchen.
