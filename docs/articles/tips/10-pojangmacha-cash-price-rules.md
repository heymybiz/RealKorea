## 💡 Local Insider Tip Boxes

### 💵 Cash Payment Requirement
Temporary street tents lack wired card terminals. Domestic patrons use instant mobile bank transfer (계좌이체). Because tourists cannot perform domestic Korean bank wires without local ID, carry 30,000–50,000 KRW in cash.

### 🍢 1-Dish-Per-Table Minimum Order
Pojangmacha are evening drinking pubs rather than cheap snack carts. Every table is expected to order at least one hot stir-fried dish (Anju) starting from 15,000–20,000 KRW alongside drinks.

### 🍲 Complimentary Eomuk Broth Refills
Every tent provides complimentary bowls of steaming radish fish cake broth (Eomuk-guk). Staff will cheerfully refill your broth throughout your evening.

## 📖 Article Content

### 1. The Street Tent Dining Experience

The orange vinyl tents lining Jongno 3-ga Station (around Exits 3, 4, and 6) and Euljiro are iconic symbols of Seoul night culture. While visitors sometimes expect modest snack prices, a Pojangmacha (포장마차) is a full evening drinking establishment.

Signature hot dishes (spicy stir-fried hagfish, pork cartilage, gizzard, sea snails, and seafood pajeon) range between 15,000 and 25,000 KRW per plate. A casual outing for two with a couple of dishes and drinks generally totals 40,000 to 60,000 KRW.

### 2. Payment Etiquette & Cash Readiness

Because mobile stalls operate without merchant card lines, local customers transfer money via mobile banking apps directly to the owner's bank account posted inside the tent. Prepare physical Korean Won banknotes before claiming a plastic stool.
