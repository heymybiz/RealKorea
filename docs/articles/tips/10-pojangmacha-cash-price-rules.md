## 💡 Local Insider Tip Boxes

### 💵 Cash-Only Operation
Street tent bars operate without fixed landline POS credit card terminals. Prepare 30,000–50,000 KRW in physical cash before sitting down on the plastic stools.

### 🍢 1-Dish-Per-Table Minimum
You cannot sit down at a pojangmacha just to order a single bottle of soju and snack on free broth. Every table must order at least one hot stir-fried main dish (Anju) starting from 15,000 KRW.

### 🍲 Complimentary Eomuk Broth
Every tent provides free bowls of steaming fish cake radish broth (Eomuk-guk). Staff will refill your hot broth for free throughout your drinking session.

## 📖 Article Content

### 1. The K-Drama Romance vs. Real Street Tent Economics

The glowing orange vinyl tents lining the streets around Jongno 3-ga Station (Exits 3, 4, and 6) and Euljiro are iconic symbols of Seoul night culture. Travelers envision eating cheap 3,000 KRW street food under the plastic tarps.

In reality, a Pojangmacha (포장마차) is not a cheap snack cart—it is a full-fledged open-air drinking pub. Main stir-fried dishes (such as spicy stir-fried hagfish, chicken gizzard, spicy pork cartilage, or grilled sea snails) range between 15,000 and 25,000 KRW per plate. A casual drinking session for two with two dishes and four bottles of soju/beer quickly tallies 45,000 to 60,000 KRW.

### 2. The Bank Transfer (Gyejwa-Iche) Culture

Because street tents are mobile temporary structures, they lack merchant card terminals. Domestic Korean customers pay using instant mobile bank transfer (계좌이체) by scanning the owner's bank account number taped to the tent roof frame.

Since international tourists cannot execute domestic Korean bank wires without an ARC card, you must hand over physical Korean Won cash banknotes. Always check your wallet before claiming a stool.
