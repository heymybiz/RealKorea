## 💡 Local Insider Tip Boxes

### ⚡ The Dual-Wallet Separation Trap
WOWPASS contains two separate, unconnected chips: an IC chip for retail shopping, and an RFID T-money chip for transit. Top-ups made with foreign currency at automated WOWPASS kiosks only load the retail shopping wallet. You must withdraw physical KRW bills and feed them into a subway station kiosk to charge the transit chip.

### 💵 Card Issuance & Fee Breakdown
Standard T-money card: 4,000–5,000 KRW (no balance, zero reload fee). WOWPASS: 5,000 KRW membership setup fee (supports in-app lock and 16 foreign currencies). NAMANE: 7,000 KRW (allows custom personal photo printing on front face).

## 📖 Article Content

### 1. The Three Card Contenders Explained

International travelers in South Korea are frequently marketed three distinct payment products: standard T-money, WOWPASS, and NAMANE. While standard Visa and Mastercard credit cards work at 98% of merchant counters, public transit (subways and city buses) relies exclusively on physical Korean RFID payment chips.

### 2. WOWPASS: The All-In-One Currency Machine

WOWPASS allows travelers to insert USD, EUR, JPY, or TWD bills directly into orange airport kiosks to receive a pre-loaded debit card loaded with KRW. It eliminates bank counter lines and offers an English mobile app for tracking expenses and locking lost cards. However, the initial 5,000 KRW membership issuance fee and the requirement to reload the transit chip with physical won banknotes remain important friction points to consider.

### 3. Best Choice by Travel Style

If you already carry a 0% foreign transaction fee credit card (Wise, Revolut, Chase Sapphire), buy a basic 4,000 KRW T-money card at the airport convenience store and load 20,000 KRW cash. Use your credit card for all restaurants and shopping, and T-money exclusively for subways. If your home bank charges high 3% overseas foreign swipe fees or you carry large stacks of cash in foreign banknotes, WOWPASS offers significant convenience.
